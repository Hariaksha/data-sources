# Disaster Dollar Database

**Theme:** Climate / Development / Public Finance / Governance
**Coverage:** United States, September 2003–present
**Unit of observation:** Federally declared major disaster incident
**Temporal granularity:** Incident-level; updated approximately twice per year
**Format:** Interactive web database; downloadable CSV in ZIP archive
**Access:** [https://carnegieendowment.org/features/disaster-dollar-database?lang=en](https://carnegieendowment.org/features/disaster-dollar-database?lang=en) — free / no registration required
**License:** Not clearly stated on the database pages; verify Carnegie Endowment terms before redistribution
**Last verified:** June 2026

***

## Key Variables

| Variable | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| `incident_id` | FEMA major disaster incident number used to identify a disaster |
| `incident_type` | Disaster type, such as hurricane, wildfire, flood, tornado, or winter storm |
| `state` | State associated with the disaster incident |
| `incident_date` | Date or timing of the disaster event |
| `fema_ihp` | FEMA Individuals and Households Program funding for the incident |
| `fema_pa` | FEMA Public Assistance funding for the incident |
| `hud_cdbg_dr` | HUD Community Development Block Grant–Disaster Recovery funding linked to the incident |
| `sba_disaster_loans` | SBA disaster loan funding associated with the incident |
| `federal_register_notice` | Linked HUD Federal Register notice when CDBG-DR funding is involved |
| `disaster_declaration_page` | Linked FEMA disaster declaration page for the incident |

## Potential Research Questions

- How is federal disaster recovery spending distributed across states, disaster types, and time?
- Which disasters receive large FEMA Public Assistance awards but little or no HUD CDBG-DR support?
- How do funding patterns differ between hurricanes, wildfires, floods, tornadoes, and winter storms?
- What does the incident-level distribution of disaster aid reveal about climate risk, adaptation, and fiscal exposure in the United States?
- Can disaster recovery allocations be linked to demographic or housing indicators to study equity in recovery support?
- How often do major disasters receive support from all four tracked federal programs versus only one or two?

## Notes & Quirks

- The database is a free, non-partisan Carnegie research tool tracking federal disaster recovery spending in the United States.
- It currently covers four major federal programs: FEMA Individuals and Households Program, FEMA Public Assistance, HUD CDBG-DR, and SBA Disaster Loans.
- The site states that data are current as of May 2026.
- Version history on the feature page says the June 2026 update expanded coverage to 1,354 incidents from September 2003 through May 2026.
- The original August 2024 release covered only 170 incidents from January 2015 through May 2024; later versions expanded scope substantially.
- FEMA IHP values reflect expended funding, while FEMA PA, HUD CDBG-DR, and SBA figures reflect obligated funding rather than necessarily final outlays.
- When one HUD allocation covers multiple incidents in a state, Carnegie splits the value evenly across covered incidents in the database.
- The database includes only natural disasters with a presidential major disaster declaration; it excludes events without major declarations, non-eligible disaster types such as extreme heat, and non-natural emergencies such as COVID-19 or chemical spills.
- The database does not include private insurance payouts, National Flood Insurance Program payments, state or local spending, military disaster spending, or broader indirect economic losses.
- Carnegie provides a downloadable ZIP file containing the full dataset and a README with methodology notes.

## How to Access

1. Visit [https://carnegieendowment.org/features/disaster-dollar-database?lang=en](https://carnegieendowment.org/features/disaster-dollar-database?lang=en).
2. Use the single-disaster lookup or database explorer to filter and compare incidents.
3. Download the full dataset from Carnegie's ZIP archive link on the feature page.
4. Consult the included README file in the download for methodological notes, source definitions, and known limitations.
5. For narrative background on the project, see Carnegie's research article: [https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/08/disaster-dollar-database](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/08/disaster-dollar-database).

## Suggested Uses

- Incident-level analysis of U.S. federal disaster recovery finance.
- Linking disaster spending to county or state socioeconomic indicators.
- Comparing reactive disaster recovery spending across hazard types.
- Studying timing, scale, and composition of federal recovery support as part of climate adaptation research.
