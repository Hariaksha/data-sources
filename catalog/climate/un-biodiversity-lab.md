# UN Biodiversity Lab – Spatial Data for Nature, Climate, and Sustainable Development

**Theme:** Climate / Biodiversity / Sustainable Development / Geospatial  
**Coverage:** Global, with national workspaces and hundreds of spatial data layers on nature, climate change, and sustainable development [page:1][web:22][web:25]  
**Unit of observation:** Spatial layer / Pixel / Protected area / Country / Project / Workspace [page:1][web:22]  
**Temporal granularity:** Varies by dataset (historical baselines to recent updates); many layers derived from satellite time series [page:1][web:25][web:27]  
**Format:** Web GIS platform with interactive maps and curated data collections; some layers downloadable; workspace functionality for registered users [page:1][web:22][web:32]  
**Access:** https://unbiodiversitylab.org/en/ and https://map.unbiodiversitylab.org/ — free; registration required for private workspaces [page:1][web:22][web:32]  
**License:** Governed by UN Biodiversity Lab terms of use; individual datasets may have their own licenses — always verify before reuse [page:1][web:31]  
**Last verified:** June 2026

## Key Variables / Data Types

| Variable / Layer type | Description |
|------------------------|-------------|
| Global biodiversity layers | Species, ecosystems, and other indicators of biodiversity importance, drawn from global providers and science teams [page:1][web:22][web:25] |
| Protected areas & OECMs | Spatial layers for protected areas and other effective area-based conservation measures, used for GBF and SDG reporting [page:1][web:22] |
| Nature-based climate solutions | Datasets on ecosystem-based mitigation and adaptation opportunities and NbS for climate [page:1] |
| Ecosystem degradation & restoration potential | Data highlighting degraded ecosystems and areas with high restoration opportunities [page:1] |
| GBF monitoring indicators | Layers aligned with the monitoring framework of the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework [page:1] |
| National datasets | Country-uploaded or curated layers used in national planning, monitoring, and reporting [page:1][web:22][web:32] |
| ELSA outputs | Integrated spatial planning outputs from the Essential Life Support Areas (ELSA) methodology [page:1][web:26][web:29] |
| Workspaces | Secure national or project workspaces combining national and global data, with dynamic indicators and map exports [page:1][web:32] |

## Notes & Quirks

- UNBL is a **spatial data and analytics platform**, not a single dataset; it aggregates hundreds of layers from satellites, science teams, and partner initiatives. [page:1][web:22][web:25][web:27]  
- The public interface emphasizes curated **data collections** (e.g., protected areas, ecosystem restoration, nature-based solutions, GBF monitoring) and an interactive map viewer rather than bulk-download tables. [page:1][web:22]  
- Many layers originate from partner initiatives (e.g., Land & Carbon Lab, Trends.Earth, Target Tracker, SEPAL); licensing and citation requirements can differ by layer. [page:1][web:22][web:24][web:31]  
- UNBL workspaces allow governments and partners to upload national data, calculate dynamic indicators, run ELSA analyses, and export maps for official reporting; access is controlled and must be requested. [page:1][web:26][web:29][web:32]  
- Because datasets evolve over time, reproducible research should record access date, dataset names, versions, and (where possible) original source links. [page:1][web:22][web:25]  
- UNBL often mirrors data available directly from source providers; it is excellent for discovery, visualization, and quick indicators, while heavy analysis may still rely on downloading from original sources. [web:22][web:25][web:35]

## How to Access

1. Start at the main site: https://unbiodiversitylab.org/en/ for an overview, documentation, stories, and links to data collections. [page:1]  
2. Launch the map viewer at https://map.unbiodiversitylab.org/ to explore global and national spatial layers interactively. [page:1][web:22]  
3. Use **Data collections** on the main site to jump into thematic collections such as Protected Areas, Nature-based Solutions for Climate, Ecosystem Restoration, and GBF Monitoring. [page:1]  
4. Request or join a **UNBL workspace** for country-specific projects, enabling uploads of national datasets and use of the ELSA Integrated Spatial Planning Tool. [page:1][web:26][web:32]  
5. When using UNBL data in research, follow the Terms of Use and dataset-specific citation guidance, and document layer names, sources, and access dates. [page:1][web:22][web:31]

## Best Use Cases

- Building spatial indicators of biodiversity importance, protection gaps, and restoration opportunities for cross-country or subnational analysis [page:1][web:22][web:25]  
- Supporting national planning, monitoring, and reporting for the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and related targets [page:1][web:26][web:29]  
- Generating subnational exposure and vulnerability measures for environmental–economic or climate–conflict research designs [page:1][web:22][web:33]  
- Identifying nature-based solutions opportunities that co-deliver climate mitigation, adaptation, and development benefits [page:1][web:22][web:25]  
- Teaching and capacity-building on using spatial data for biodiversity and sustainable development, including via UNBL trainings and online courses. [page:1][web:27][web:33]
