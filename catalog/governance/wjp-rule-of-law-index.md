# WJP Rule of Law Index

**Theme:** Governance / Rule of Law and Justice
**Source:** World Justice Project (WJP), an independent nonprofit based in Washington, D.C.
**Coverage:** 143 countries and jurisdictions in the 2026 edition (about 95% of the world's population); published annually since 2009, with far fewer countries in early editions
**Unit of observation:** Country-year (overall score, 8 factor scores, and sub-factor scores)
**Temporal granularity:** Annual; the 2026 edition was released October 21, 2026
**Format:** Interactive rankings and country profiles, report PDFs, and downloadable score data
**Access:** [https://worldjusticeproject.org/rule-of-law-index/](https://worldjusticeproject.org/rule-of-law-index/) — free to view; the site loads through JavaScript, so open it in a browser
**License:** Not confirmed; check WJP's terms of service before redistributing the data
**Last verified:** November 2026

## What It Is

The Index measures how the rule of law is experienced and perceived in practice, rather than what laws say on paper. It draws on two original surveys: a household survey of the general public and a questionnaire for in-country legal practitioners and experts. The 2026 edition used more than 215,000 household surveys and about 3,400 expert surveys (about 4,100 in 2025).

Scores run from 0 to 1, where 1 is the strongest adherence to the rule of law. The overall score is the average of eight factors, each weighted equally:

| Factor | What it covers |
|--------|----------------|
| Constraints on Government Powers | Limits from the legislature, the judiciary, independent auditors, non-governmental checks, and lawful transitions of power |
| Absence of Corruption | Corruption in the executive, judiciary, police and military, and legislature |
| Open Government | Published laws, the right to information, civic participation, and complaint mechanisms |
| Fundamental Rights | Equal treatment, due process, and freedoms of expression, religion, privacy, assembly and association, and labor rights |
| Order and Security | Crime control, civil conflict, and people's use of violence to settle grievances |
| Regulatory Enforcement | Whether government regulations are enforced effectively, without improper influence or delay, and with due process |
| Civil Justice | Access, affordability, delays, freedom from improper influence, and alternatives to court |
| Criminal Justice | Investigations, adjudication, corrections, impartiality, and due process |

## 2026 Findings

- Declines outnumbered improvements for the ninth year in a row (62% of countries declined and 38% improved), although fewer countries declined than in 2025 (68%).
- More than 86% of countries had a sustained five-year decline in at least one of constraints on government powers, open government, or fundamental rights.
- Civil justice weakened in 64% of countries and criminal justice in 58%, with government interference a central driver in both.
- **Top five:** Denmark, Norway, Finland, Sweden, Luxembourg. **Bottom five:** Nicaragua (139), Cambodia (140), Haiti (141), Afghanistan (142), Venezuela (143).
- **Largest declines:** Haiti (-5.6%), Tanzania (-3.5%), Sudan (-2.8%). **Largest improvements:** Latvia (+1.6%), Trinidad and Tobago (+1.2%), Guatemala (+1.1%).

## Notes & Quirks

- **Perception-based.** Scores come from surveys, so they can move in response to news, conflict, or political events. That makes the Index risky as a control in models of conflict or unrest, since the outcome may shape the score.
- **Small annual changes.** Year-to-year movements are usually a few percent. Separate real change from survey noise before treating a change as an event.
- **Household surveys are not run every year in every country.** WJP refreshes its household polling on a rotating schedule, so some annual changes come mainly from the expert survey. Check the methodology for when each country was last polled.
- **Mainly urban samples.** Household polling is typically conducted in each country's largest cities, so rural experience of justice and enforcement may be underrepresented. This matters for rural or forest-frontier research questions.
- **Country-level only.** No subnational data, so it can't distinguish provinces or districts within Indonesia or other countries.
- **Changing coverage and editions.** Early editions covered far fewer countries, and the methodology has been revised. Check the historical data before building a long panel.
- **Overlap with other governance indices.** It is used as a source in other composite measures, such as the World Bank's Worldwide Governance Indicators. Don't include both as independent variables.
