# Global Innovation Index (GII)

**Theme:** Development & Wellbeing / Innovation & Technology
**Source:** World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)
**Coverage:** 139 economies (GII 2025 and 2026; about 94% of world population); annual editions since 2007
**Unit of observation:** Country (economy)
**Temporal granularity:** Annual edition (each edition draws on the latest available data, which can lag by a year or more)
**Format:** Web report and interactive rankings; indicator data downloadable from WIPO's GII pages
**Access:** [https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-innovation-index](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-innovation-index) — free
**License:** Free to use with attribution to WIPO; check WIPO's terms for each edition before redistributing indicator data
**Last verified:** October 2026

---

## What It Is

The GII ranks economies on innovation capacity and performance. The 19th edition (GII 2026) was released on September 29, 2026, with a special theme on deep-science startups. Switzerland, Sweden, the United States, South Korea, and Singapore led in both 2025 and 2026.

In the 2025 framework, 78 indicators are grouped into 21 sub-pillars and 7 pillars, then combined into a 0–100 score:

| Sub-index | Pillars |
|-----------|---------|
| **Innovation inputs** | Institutions; Human capital and research; Infrastructure; Market sophistication; Business sophistication |
| **Innovation outputs** | Knowledge and technology outputs; Creative outputs |

An economy is included only if it has data for about two-thirds of the indicators in each sub-index. The index is audited each year by the European Commission's Joint Research Centre (JRC).

The report also maps the world's top 100 **science and technology clusters** (city-level concentrations of patenting and scientific publishing).

---

## Key Variables

- Overall GII score and rank
- Innovation Input and Innovation Output sub-index scores
- Scores for the 7 pillars and 21 sub-pillars
- Underlying indicators (e.g., R&D spending, researchers per capita, patent applications, venture capital deals, high-tech exports, creative goods exports)
- Top 100 science and technology clusters (city-level)

## Potential Research Questions

- Is innovation capacity associated with how well countries adapt to climate shocks (e.g., fire or disaster losses relative to exposure)?
- Do conflict-affected or fragile states (see [Fragile States Index](../governance/fragile-states-index.md)) score systematically lower on specific pillars, such as institutions or infrastructure?
- How does Indonesia's position on the human capital and institutions pillars compare to regional peers?

## Notes & Quirks

- **Rankings are not comparable across editions.** WIPO changes indicators, weights, and country coverage between years, and warns against reading rank changes as performance changes. This makes the GII weak as a panel dataset.
- **A composite score bundles many things.** For research, the underlying indicators (R&D spending, patents, etc.) are usually more useful than the headline score, and often better obtained from their original sources (UNESCO, WIPO patent statistics, World Bank).
- **Country-level only.** No subnational data, apart from the city-level cluster rankings.
- **Data lags.** Each edition uses the latest available values, so a "2026" score can reflect data from several earlier years.
