# Aid Transparency Index (ATI)

**Theme:** Development & Wellbeing / Aid and Development Finance
**Source:** Publish What You Fund (UK-registered charity and aid transparency campaign)
**Coverage:** Major aid and development organizations; editions from 2012 to 2026. Latest edition assesses 33 organizations (50 in 2022 and 2024, 47 in 2020, 45 in 2018)
**Unit of observation:** Agency-edition (some development banks are scored separately for sovereign and non-sovereign portfolios)
**Temporal granularity:** Periodic; every two years since at least 2018. The 2026 edition was released November 4, 2026
**Format:** Interactive web rankings, agency profiles, and reports; results data, technical papers, and reports available as downloads

## What It Is

The ATI scores major aid organizations on how much information they publish about their aid and how usable it is. It measures disclosure, not whether the aid is effective or well targeted.

Information published to the [International Aid Transparency Initiative (IATI) Standard](https://iatistandard.org/) can earn the highest indicator scores. Scores are reported on a 0–100 scale, with ratings from "very poor" to "very good".

The 2026 Index uses 35 indicators in five weighted categories:

| Category | Weight |
|----------|--------|
| Organizational planning and commitments | 15% |
| Finance and budgets | 25% |
| Project attributes | 20% |
| Joining-up development data | 20% |
| Performance | 20% |

**Data collection:** For IATI publishers, automated tests in the Aid Transparency Tracker run first. A manual survey covers indicators where information isn't on the IATI Registry or fails those tests, and organizations that don't publish to IATI are assessed entirely by survey. Organizations can review their assessments, and surveys are independently reviewed. A visibility assessment compares published spending with an external source, usually OECD-DAC data, and can lower the score of an agency that publishes too little of its portfolio.

## 2026 Results

- The average score was 62.1, compared with 62.7 in 2024. Twenty of 33 organizations were rated "good" or "very good", and three "poor" or "very poor".
- Multilaterals took the top five places: African Development Bank sovereign portfolio (98.8), Inter-American Development Bank and World Bank IDA (tied at 97.1), then UNDP and UNICEF.
- The three largest donors all fell: Germany's BMZ-GIZ (-15.5 points), the UK's FCDO (-13.9), and the US State Department (48.6 to 42.6). USAID closed and is no longer in the Index.
- Spain's AECID rose 57.7 points (5.0 to 62.7), the largest gain in the Index's history. The UAE fell 41.6 points after it stopped publishing regularly.
- Performance information remained the weakest category, averaging 7.5 out of 20.

## Potential Research Questions

- Do [GODAD](godad.md) geocoding gaps, by donor and year, track that donor's ATI score? This would show whether missing locations reflect donor disclosure.
- Did agencies that became more transparent see changes in the geographic targeting of their aid, or does better reporting only make existing allocation visible?
- When a donor's score falls sharply, does that coincide with cuts in aid? The 2024–2026 changes in the US, Germany, the UK, and the UAE are possible cases, though with very few observations.
- Are aid-dependent countries served by more transparent donors better able to plan their budgets?

## Notes & Quirks

- **Paid accreditation since 2026.** The Index was philanthropically funded from 2012 to 2024. In 2026 it relaunched with a paid model after funding ran out: 17 agencies signed up to pay for assessment, engagement, and capacity building, and major bilateral donors were included without paying. Publish What You Fund says all are scored with the same method, but only paying agencies get a practice round and detailed feedback, which could raise their scores.
- **Sample change in 2026.** Coverage fell from 50 to 33 organizations, so the 2024 and 2026 averages don't compare the same agencies. Compare within agencies, or restrict to a balanced panel.
- **Opt-outs.** First-time agencies can choose not to be ranked in their first year; the Green Climate Fund and the Hewlett Foundation did so in 2026.
- **Canceled, then revived.** Coverage from March 2025 [reports the 2026 edition as canceled](https://publishwhatyoufund.org/2025/03/global-aid-dataset-at-risk-with-cancellation-of-2026-aid-transparency-index). Those reports are out of date. Future editions depend on the accreditation model.
- **Methodology changes.** The method was updated for the 2018, 2020, and 2024 editions and is revised between editions, so treat long-run score changes with caution. The comparison chart covers 2013–2024 and doesn't include 2026.
- **Very small sample.** With one row per agency per edition and no subnational data, it works better as a donor-level measure of data quality than as a main outcome.
- **Rewards a format as well as disclosure.** For 22 indicators, the score depends on data format and consistency, and machine-readable data scores above PDFs. A low score doesn't always mean an agency's information is unavailable.
- **Produced by an advocacy organization.** Publish What You Fund campaigns for the transparency it measures, and IATI, whose standard it rewards, helped fund the 2024 edition.
