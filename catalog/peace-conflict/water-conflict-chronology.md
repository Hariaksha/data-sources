# Water Conflict Chronology

**Theme:** Peace & Conflict / Water and Climate Security
**Source:** Pacific Institute (Oakland, California); maintained by Peter Gleick and Morgan Shimabuku
**Coverage:** Global; about 4,500 years (ancient Mesopotamia to present), with most entries after 2000. Over 2,750 incidents as of the November 2025 update
**Unit of observation:** Incident (one event of violence involving water resources or water systems)
**Temporal granularity:** Event dates (precision varies); database updated periodically (2024 and November 2025 updates)
**Format:** Interactive map and list view; CSV download
**Access:** [Interactive map](https://worldwater.org/conflict/map/) · [Overview and download](https://www.worldwater.org/water-conflict/) — free for non-commercial use, no registration

## What It Is

The Water Conflict Chronology is the most comprehensive open list of violence involving water resources and water systems. The Pacific Institute has maintained it since the 1980s. The November 2025 update added 844 new incidents.

Each incident is classified by the role water played (an incident can have more than one):

| Category | Meaning | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Trigger** | A dispute over access to or control of water led to violence | Herder–farmer clashes over a water point |
| **Casualty / target** | Water resources or systems were attacked or damaged | Bombing of a dam, pipeline, or treatment plant |
| **Weapon** | Water or water systems were used as a tool of violence | Cutting off supply to a besieged city; deliberate flooding |

Its headline finding is a sharp rise in reported water-related violence since the early 2000s. 2023 was a record year, driven especially by attacks on water infrastructure, including in Ukraine and Gaza.

**Methodology:** Gleick, P. & Shimabuku, M. (2023). [Water-related conflicts: definitions, data, and trends from the water conflict chronology](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/acbb8f). *Environmental Research Letters*.

## Key Variables

- Date of the incident
- Location (country, region, and approximate coordinates for the map)
- Category: trigger, casualty/target, and/or weapon
- Whether people died (yes/no; no fatality counts)
- Region (17 world regions)
- Short description of the incident
- Source citation(s)

## Potential Research Questions

- Do droughts or rainfall shocks ([ERA5](../climate/era5-wind.md) and other climate data) increase the incidence of water-triggered violence, as opposed to attacks on water infrastructure?
- How often do water-triggered incidents in this database appear in [ACLED](acled.md) or [UCDP](ucdp.md), and how do those datasets code them? This would show how much water-related violence general conflict datasets capture.
- Are attacks on water infrastructure concentrated in particular conflict phases (e.g., sieges, urban warfare)?
- Do countries flagged as high-risk by the [Climate Conflict Vulnerability Index](climate-conflict-vulnerability-index.md) or [Water Resource Vulnerability Monitor](../climate/water-resource-vulnerability.md) actually see more water-related violence?

## Notes & Quirks

- **Based on media and secondary sources.** Reporting is uneven across countries and over time. Part of the recent rise likely reflects more news coverage and more searching by the compilers (844 incidents were added in one update), not only more violence.
- **Not a consistent sample over time.** Ancient anecdotes and modern news reports sit together, with no fixed rules for inclusion over the whole period. Long-run trend analysis is not meaningful; restrict to recent decades and still be cautious.
- **Approximate locations.** The map notes that locations "may not be exact." Check events by hand before any subnational analysis.
- **Limited detail.** No actor coding, fatality counts, or location-precision codes of the kind [UCDP](ucdp.md) and [ACLED](acled.md) provide — only whether deaths occurred.
- **Best used as a case list.** A practical approach is to use it to identify water-related incidents and their category, then match them to ACLED or UCDP events for actors, fatalities, and precise locations.
- **Non-commercial license.** Fine for academic research with attribution; redistribution in commercial products is not allowed.
